How we brought students back to the club

The aivancity Tech Club runs hackathons, coding sessions and tech events. Yet when we asked 100 students about it this autumn, 74 of them either had never heard of the club or didn't know what it does. Only 8 had ever attended an activity.

By Victoria Alajemba and Célian Faucille · 7 November 2026

  • 74 / 100students didn't know the club or what it does
  • 47 / 100didn't know when or where activities happen
  • 7 → 19event registrations after the new site

So we set out to answer a simple question: why don't more students take part, and what would bring them in?

What students told us

We interviewed 13 students and ran a survey that 100 students answered between 8 October and 4 November 2026. We expected to hear that the activities weren't interesting enough. That's not what they said.

The biggest barrier was much simpler. 47 students said they don't know when or where activities happen. After that came lack of time (38) and not knowing anyone in the club (27). Only 4 said the activities didn't match their interests.

In other words, students weren't saying no to the Tech Club. Most of them never had the chance to say yes.

What we built

If the problem is information, the fix is one place where everything is easy to find. So we rebuilt the club website, aivancity-tech-club.github.io, around three things:

  • A clear schedule of upcoming events, with dates, times, places and the language each activity runs in
  • Online registration, so joining the club or signing up for an event takes a minute
  • Club Pulse, a page that shows how many members have registered, so we can see whether the club is growing

A website only helps if people see it. Our survey showed that students notice email (43) and posters on campus (41) far more than any website (12). So we put a poster with a QR code on the school's vertical screens and sent an email to every student.

The results so far

We asked 10 students to try the new site and fill in a feedback form, most of them inactive or rarely active members. They rated the club's usefulness 7.3 out of 10 on average, and 9 out of 10 said they were satisfied. One tester said it used to be "very hard to know what they were organising", but now it is intuitive.

The clearest sign came from registrations. Our last event before the new site had 7 sign-ups. Our next event, on 12 November, already has 19, almost three times as many.

What we learned

  • Ask before you build. We nearly started by planning new activities. The survey showed the real problem was that students couldn't find the ones we already had.
  • Go where people already look. A good website is invisible until a poster or an email points to it.
  • Respect students' time. Time was the second biggest barrier, and 42 students said they're more likely to come if they can drop in and leave freely. That will shape our next events.
